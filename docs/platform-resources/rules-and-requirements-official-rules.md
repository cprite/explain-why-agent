## 1. Purpose

These Official Rules govern participation in the **Open Agent Hackathon 2026**. They apply to all participants, teams, organizers, mentors, judges, sponsors and platform administrators.

By registering for or participating in the hackathon, each participant confirms they have read, understood and agreed to these rules together with all published submission requirements, technical requirements and the code of conduct.

The hackathon exists to surface AI agents that do real work: agents that can access relevant information, understand context and relationships, reason across multiple steps, maintain useful context, and turn that understanding into a useful outcome.

## 5. Teams

Participants may take part as:

1. **An individual.**
2. **A pre-formed team**, registered together.
3. **A participant who forms or joins a team on HackOS** before the build window opens.

### 5.1 Team size

Teams may have **1 to 5 members**.

### 5.2 One submission per participant

Each participant may appear on **only one submission**. A participant may not be a member of more than one competing team.

### 5.3 Team contact

Each team should designate one primary contact responsible for coordinating the submission, confirming the final roster and receiving official communications.

### 5.4 Roster changes

Teams may change membership during the build window. The final roster must be accurate at the submission deadline. After the deadline, roster changes are not permitted except to correct an obvious administrative error, with organizer approval.

### 5.5 Team disputes

Teams are responsible for resolving internal matters: division of work, use of shared code, attribution, decision-making and how any prize is shared. The organizers may help with platform or administrative issues but do not adjudicate internal disputes unless conduct, eligibility, IP or rule violations are involved.

### 5.6 Prizes and team size

Team size does not change the prize. Prizes are split equally among team members by default.

## 6. Challenge Tracks

Each participant or team selects **one** of four tracks and builds for it.

| # | Track | What you build |
|---:|---|---|
| 1 | **Fragmented** | Agents that discover and query information scattered across disconnected systems, APIs, databases and data sources, and turn it into actionable insights, decisions, reports or recommendations. |
| 2 | **Autonomous Intelligence** | Agents that investigate complex questions across multiple data sources, connect findings, and produce evidence-backed answers or recommendations. |
| 3 | **Persistent Memory Agents** | Agents that remember previous interactions, maintain state, and continue useful work across longer-running tasks or workflows. |
| 4 | **Real-World Industry Agents** | Agents designed for a specific industry or community, solving a concrete operational problem that could realistically be used in practice. |

Full track statements are published on HackOS. Submissions must map clearly to the selected track.

## 7. What You Build

### 7.1 Objective

Build a working agent that does real work for a defined user. This is not a prompt-writing contest or a UI showcase.

### 7.2 What a submission should demonstrate

- A real problem and a clear user
- An agent that acts rather than describes
- Evidence of how the system reached its output
- A result that runs from a clean setup
- Use of the partner stack where it genuinely helps

**[VERIFY: the minimum technical requirements in section 6 of the published rules, including any required submission components.]**

### 7.3 What does not meet the bar

- A fixed chain with no feedback, critique or validation
- Agents that are prompt wrappers, or one agent with several labels
- Hardcoded outputs or fabricated agent activity
- A UI with no working system behind it

## 11. Data and Security

### 11.1 Permitted

- Publicly available datasets with appropriate licences
- Small static datasets committed to the repository
- Automated download scripts that need no login or manual steps
- Public APIs called at runtime
- Synthetic or simulated data, clearly identified

### 11.2 Not permitted

- Confidential, proprietary or restricted data the participant is not authorised to use
- Personal data without authorisation and appropriate safeguards
- Committed API keys, passwords, tokens, certificates or credentials
- Datasets used in breach of their licence terms
- Malware, exploit code, credential harvesting or harmful automation

### 11.3 Reproducibility

Data loading should be automated and documented. Submissions should avoid heavy downloads at runtime and should provide a fallback or sample mode.

## 14. Judging

### 14.1 Process

Judging opens **20 October 2026, 00:00 UTC** and runs while final submissions come in. Submissions are reviewed by expert judges against the published criteria.

**[VERIFY: whether judging runs in stages, who the judges are, and whether feedback is provided to participants.]**

### 14.2 Criteria

| Criterion | Points | What judges look for |
|---|---:|---|
| **Impact** | 30 | A real problem, a clear user, and an output that improves a decision or workflow |
| **Technical** | 20 | A functional working system with effective use of tools, APIs, models and data |
| **Innovation** | 15 | Novel approach and creative use of agent design patterns |
| **Demo** | 15 | Clarity of explanation and quality of the demonstration |
| **Product & UX** | 10 | Whether the output is usable by the person it is meant for |
| **Sponsor Tech** | 10 | Meaningful use of the partner stack where it helps the build |
| **Total** | **100** | |

Ties for third place are decided on the **Impact** score.

### 14.3 Decisions

Judge and organizer decisions are final on eligibility, disqualification, scoring, winner selection and prize eligibility. The organizers are not required to disclose detailed scoring or judge deliberations.

## 16. Disqualification

A submission may be rejected or disqualified if it:

- Is submitted after the deadline
- Cannot be run or evaluated
- Uses hardcoded outputs or contains no real agent logic
- Shows no evidence of how the agent reached its output
- Commits API keys, tokens, credentials or secrets
- Includes unauthorised confidential, personal or restricted data
- Uses data, code, models or assets without permission
- Contains malicious code
- Misrepresents the submission, team, authorship, functionality or results
- Comes from duplicate accounts, or a participant listed on more than one team
- Violates the code of conduct or these rules

The organizers may request clarification before disqualifying, but are not required to.

## 17. Code of Conduct

Participants must act professionally and respectfully.

Not permitted: harassment, discrimination, intimidation or abusive behaviour; offensive communications; disruption of channels, workshops or judging; unauthorised access to systems, accounts or repositories; credential sharing; interference with another team's work; plagiarism or misrepresentation; unsafe, unlawful or unethical AI behaviour.

## 18. Responsible AI

Participants should build responsibly. Where relevant, submissions should include input validation, safe handling of user content, clearly stated limitations, human review for high-risk outputs, disclaimers for medical, legal, financial or safety-sensitive use cases, logging and traceability, and error handling.

Submissions should avoid harmful, deceptive, discriminatory or unsafe outputs, and should not take unauthorised autonomous actions.
