# Code of Conduct

**OPEN AGENT HACKATHON 2026 | Build an agent that does real work**

The Open Agent Hackathon is a global online event, free and open to builders worldwide. This Code of Conduct sets the standard for how we treat each other.

Participating means agreeing to follow it in every official event space: HackOS, its channels, mentor office hours, team-finding, submissions, demos, judging interactions, sponsor workshops, the **21 October participant onboarding**, the **results call on 6 November**, and the community on Discord.

---

## 1. Purpose

Six days is not long, and the event only works if everyone can take part without harassment, intimidation, discrimination or disruption.

This Code of Conduct exists to:

- Protect participants, mentors, judges, sponsors, organizers and support staff
- Set clear expectations for behaviour
- Define what is unacceptable
- Give people a practical way to report a problem
- Explain how organizers respond

---

## 2. Who It Applies To

Everyone: participants and team members, solo builders, mentors, judges, organizers, sponsor representatives, speakers, support staff and invited guests.

The standard is the same regardless of experience, seniority, country, team or role. A judge or a sponsor is held to it exactly as a first-time participant is.

---

## 3. Where It Applies

All official event spaces, including:

- HackOS: profiles, team pages, project pages, channels, comments and messages
- The #help desk channel and the sponsor Help channels
- Announcements and team-finding
- Mentor office hours
- Sponsor workshops
- Participant onboarding, 21 October
- The results call, 6 November
- The community on Discord
- Repositories submitted to the hackathon, including READMEs, issues, commits and pull requests
- Demo videos and slide decks
- Judging and feedback interactions

Behaviour outside these spaces may be reviewed if it affects participant safety, event integrity or another team's ability to take part.

---

## 4. Expected Behaviour

### 4.1 Be respectful

- Treat people with courtesy and patience
- Assume good intent where reasonable
- Respect different working styles, technical backgrounds, time zones, cultures and levels of English
- Give feedback on ideas, code and architecture, not on people
- Use inclusive, professional language

This is a worldwide event across many time zones and first languages. Someone writing in their third language at 3am is doing something hard. Read generously.

### 4.2 Be collaborative

- Help others without taking over their work
- Share resources, debugging tips and things you learned
- Ask questions clearly
- Keep discussion relevant to the channel you are in
- Credit others for ideas, snippets and contributions
- Communicate early if your availability changes

### 4.3 Be constructive

Strong technical disagreement is welcome. Personal attacks are not.

**Acceptable:**

> "I think this may struggle on the Technical score because the agents only run in sequence. Adding a feedback loop between the evaluator and the planner would show more."

**Not acceptable:**

> "This is obviously bad. You don't understand agents."

When reviewing someone's work: be specific, explain your reasoning, suggest an improvement, and skip the sarcasm.

### 4.4 Use the right channel

| Topic | Where |
|---|---|
| Track scope and expectations | Challenge pages on HackOS |
| General technical questions | #help desk channel on HackOS |
| Sponsor technology setup and errors | Sponsor folder in Resources, then that sponsor's Help channel (Help Zetaris, Help Meterless, Help Cursor, Help NVIDIA) |
| Agent design, frameworks, debugging | Mentor office hours |
| Rules and submission requirements | Official Rules on the event page, or hackathons@genai.works |
| Finding teammates | Team-finding on HackOS |
| Official updates | Announcements on HackOS |

Don't post the same question in several channels unless a moderator asks you to.

### 4.5 Protect credentials and data

**Do:** keep your API keys private, use environment variables, strip keys and sensitive records from any log you share, use properly licensed data, and report an exposed key immediately.

**Do not:** post keys, credentials, tokens or private endpoints; share confidential or personal data you are not authorised to share; access another participant's account, repository or submission without permission; or use the event to test offensive security techniques against HackOS, the sponsors, other participants or any third-party system.

## 5. Unacceptable Behaviour

### 5.1 Harassment and discrimination

Any unwelcome conduct that creates an intimidating, hostile, humiliating or offensive environment, including:

- Offensive comments related to gender, gender identity, race, ethnicity, nationality, religion, age, disability, appearance, sexual orientation, experience level or background
- Slurs, stereotypes, insults or degrading jokes
- Repeated unwelcome contact
- Intimidation, threats or coercion
- Mocking someone's accent, language ability, writing or skill level
- Sexual comments, images or unwelcome sexual attention
- Deliberate misgendering, or using a name someone has asked you not to use
- Publishing or threatening to publish someone's private information

### 5.2 Bullying and personal attacks

Insults, name-calling, ridicule, aggressive messages, hostile interruption in live sessions, piling on after a mistake, repeatedly targeting a person or team, deliberately derailing conversations, or criticism designed to embarrass rather than help.

### 5.3 Retaliation

Nobody may punish, threaten, exclude or mistreat someone because they reported a concern, helped someone report one, took part in a review, or asked someone to stop.

Retaliation may result in immediate removal from the event.

### 5.4 Disrupting event spaces

Spamming, flooding support channels, irrelevant promotion, hijacking Q&A, interrupting speakers, presenting misleading information as official guidance, or impersonating organizers, moderators, judges, mentors or sponsors.

### 5.5 Misconduct in submissions

- Plagiarism, or presenting someone else's work as your own
- Taking code, prompts, architecture or documentation from another team without permission
- Sabotaging another team's work or repository
- Pressuring a teammate to give up authorship, credit or a share of a prize
- Misrepresenting who contributed
- Failing to declare pre-existing work
- Submitting malicious code, spyware, credential harvesting or hidden functionality
- Including offensive or unsafe content in demos, slide decks, logs, prompts, outputs or documentation

### 5.6 Unsafe AI or data behaviour

- Building systems to generate targeted harassment, hate speech, non-consensual sexual content or abusive content
- Building systems to deceive, to impersonate real people without authorisation, or to manipulate participants
- Using sensitive personal data, health data, private financial data or confidential material you are not authorised to use
- Attempting to bypass sponsor platform controls, quotas or access boundaries
- Sharing model outputs or datasets in breach of licences or privacy expectations

## 6. Live Sessions

Onboarding on 21 October, sponsor workshops, mentor office hours and the results call are official event spaces, and the same standards apply.

**During a session:** join with your real name or your event profile name, keep your microphone muted unless invited to speak, keep questions relevant, follow moderator instructions, and never post keys or credentials in chat. Don't record or screenshot other participants without permission.

**Moderators may:** remove chat messages, mute participants, disable chat, move questions to the right channel, remove someone from the session, and escalate serious concerns.

---

## 7. Reporting a Concern

Report it. You don't need to decide whether it counts as a formal violation first. If something feels unsafe, discriminatory, harassing or wrong, tell a moderator or organizer.

### 7.1 Urgent concerns

For threats, doxxing, leaked credentials, malicious code, a suspected account compromise or harassment happening live:

1. Leave the situation if you can
2. Tell a moderator or organizer immediately, by direct message on HackOS
3. If you can't reach one, email **hackathons@genai.works**

### 7.2 Standard reporting

1. **HackOS reporting tools**, where available
2. **A private message** to a moderator, mentor or organizer on HackOS
3. **Email** hackathons@genai.works
4. **During a live session**, use chat, Q&A or a direct message to reach the moderator

If you would rather not describe the problem publicly, you don't have to:

> "I need to report a Code of Conduct concern privately. Could a moderator contact me?"

### 7.3 What to include

Where you can: your name and how to reach you, who was involved, when and where it happened, what happened, any screenshots or links, whether it is ongoing, whether anyone is at immediate risk, and what outcome you are hoping for.

Report even if you don't have all of this.

---

## 8. How Reports Are Handled

Organizers will acknowledge the report, assess whether immediate action is needed, review the evidence, speak with the people involved where appropriate, decide whether the Code of Conduct was violated, act, and record the outcome.

### 8.1 Confidentiality

Reports are shared only with people who need to know in order to respond. Confidentiality cannot be guaranteed absolutely; some situations require escalation to platform providers, sponsors or, in serious cases, law enforcement.

### 8.2 Response timing

| Severity | Example | Response |
|---|---|---|
| **Urgent** | Threats, harassment in progress, leaked credentials, malicious code | Immediate moderator attention |
| **High** | Discrimination, repeated targeting, serious team misconduct | Prioritised review |
| **Standard** | Rude comments, channel misuse, minor disruption | Moderator review and correction |
| **Low** | Misplaced post, off-topic discussion | Redirect or reminder |

The event runs 144 hours across every time zone. Moderators are not awake at all hours, but urgent reports are prioritised.

---

## 9. Consequences

Organizers may take whatever action is needed to protect participants and the integrity of the event.

### 9.1 Corrective

A reminder, a request to edit or remove a message, a redirect to the right channel, or a warning about tone or conduct.

### 9.2 Moderation

Removing posts or files, a temporary mute, a posting restriction, removal from a channel or a live session, a direct-message restriction, or a temporary suspension of HackOS access.

### 9.3 Event-level

For serious or repeated violations: a formal warning, removal from a team, **disqualification from the hackathon**, loss of HackOS and community access, revocation of sponsor credentials where misuse is involved, exclusion from judging and prize consideration, and a ban from future GenAI Works events.

### 9.4 Immediate removal

Threats or intimidation, harassment or discrimination, retaliation, exposing credentials or private data, malicious code or security abuse, deliberate sabotage of another team, impersonating an organizer or judge, or repeatedly refusing moderator instructions.

## 10. Teams

### 10.1 Agree early

Teams form fast here, sometimes between strangers who met on HackOS days earlier. Before you start building, agree on: who does what, how decisions get made, how credit appears in the README, **how any prize is split**, and what happens if someone drops out.

Prizes are split equally among team members by default. If your team wants something else, agree it in writing before you submit, not after you win.

### 10.2 Credit

**Do:** credit teammates accurately, cite libraries, datasets and examples, document open-source components and licences, and be transparent about AI-assisted work.

**Do not:** remove someone's credit without discussion, claim sole authorship of team work, or copy another team's implementation, prompts, architecture or demo.

### 10.3 Conflict

If you can't resolve it internally: pause the conversation, write down what happened factually, and ask a moderator for help. Raise membership issues **before your first submission is created**, since your team locks at that point, and raise any other submission issue before the deadline.

Organizers help with platform and administrative problems. They don't adjudicate who contributed more.

---

## 11. Asking for Help Well

Include what you're trying to do, what you expected, what actually happened, the error message, your framework and stack, whether it fails locally or in Docker, and sanitised logs.

**Good:**

> "I'm using LangGraph with Meterless. The model call works locally but fails in Docker with a 401. The key is passed as an environment variable. Sanitised error below. Any ideas?"

**Not useful:**

> "Nothing works. Please fix."

**Never post** API keys, real `.env` files, access tokens, passwords, repository credentials or logs containing personal data. If you post a secret by accident, delete it and tell a moderator immediately, then rotate the key.

**Be patient.** Support comes from mentors, organizers and sponsor teams across time zones, and response times stretch during the final 24 hours. Don't repeatedly tag or direct-message several people about the same issue unless it's urgent.

---

## 12. Mentors, Judges and Organizers

They follow this Code of Conduct too, and are expected to:

- Treat all participants fairly
- Declare conflicts of interest
- Keep submissions and evaluation details confidential
- Give feedback professionally
- Avoid favouritism, including the appearance of it
- Never request access to participant credentials or private systems
- Never use a participant's ideas, code or materials outside the event without permission

Mentors help you understand the tracks, navigate HackOS and the sponsor technologies, and get unstuck. They do not build your project, and they must not give one team an advantage they would not give another.

Report concerns about a mentor, judge or organizer through the same paths in section 7.

---

## 13. Submission Content

Repositories, READMEs, slide decks, logs, demo videos, generated outputs, screenshots and sample data must all be appropriate for a public event.

Submissions must not contain: harassing, discriminatory or abusive content; unnecessary personal data; exposed secrets; malicious or hidden destructive code; or misleading claims about capabilities, authorship, data sources, sponsor technology use or results.

---

## 14. Privacy and Recording

**Do not:** screenshot private messages for public posting without consent, except when reporting misconduct; record live sessions unless permitted; share recordings, chat transcripts or participant lists outside the event; publish someone's personal information; or use someone's image, voice, name or likeness in a demo without permission.

When reporting misconduct, sharing screenshots privately with moderators is fine.

---

## 15. Good-Faith Reports

Report in good faith, meaning you honestly believe there may be a problem, even if the review finds there wasn't one.

Knowingly false, malicious or retaliatory reports are themselves a violation.

---

## 16. If You Are Asked to Stop

1. Stop immediately
2. Don't argue in the channel or the session
3. If you think it's a misunderstanding, raise it privately with a moderator
4. Don't retaliate against the person who raised it

A correction early prevents an escalation later.

---

## 17. Examples

**Technical disagreement**

Acceptable: "I disagree with a single orchestrator for every step, because it hides whether the agents really collaborate. Could you show evaluator-to-planner feedback in the logs?"

Not acceptable: "This design is amateur. You clearly haven't built real systems."

**Asking for help**

Acceptable: "Could someone clarify what counts as evidence of how the agent reached its output? I've read the submission requirements and want to check whether a JSONL trace is enough."

Not acceptable: "The rules are confusing. Whoever wrote this needs to fix it now."

**Live session chat**

Acceptable: "Question for the Zetaris team: if we hit a connection limit during the demo, should we ask in #help desk or the Help Zetaris channel?"

Not acceptable: "This platform is useless. Nobody here knows what they're doing."

**Team conflict**

Acceptable: "We disagree on the final architecture and need help clarifying submission credit before the deadline."

Not acceptable: "My teammate is incompetent and should be removed."

**Security slip**

Acceptable: "I accidentally committed an API key. I've removed it and rotated the key. Anything else I should do?"

Not acceptable: Posting the key in a public channel and asking whether it still works.

## 18. Your Responsibilities

- Follow this Code of Conduct
- Read and follow the Official Rules
- Use the right channels
- Respect moderator decisions
- Report serious concerns
- Protect credentials and confidential information
- Treat everyone as a collaborator, including the teams you're competing against

---

## 19. Organizer Responsibilities

- Set the tone
- Monitor official spaces
- Respond to reports
- Remove inappropriate content
- Apply consequences consistently and proportionately
- Protect confidentiality where possible
- Keep the event fair and safe

---

## 20. Final Word

The strongest teams here will show technical depth, agents that do real work, respectful communication, responsible use of AI, security-conscious development and real value for someone.

You're competing for a prize pool, but you're building alongside thousands of people doing the same thing in the same six days. Treat them accordingly.
