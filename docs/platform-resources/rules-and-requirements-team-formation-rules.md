# Team Formation Rules

**OPEN AGENT HACKATHON 2026 | Build an agent that does real work**

How to build alone, form a team, join a team, change a roster and confirm your final team for submission. Written to prevent disputes and keep evaluation fair.

**Applies to:** all registered participants
**Platform:** HackOS
**Build window:** 22–27 October 2026, 144 hours
**Submissions close:** **27 October 2026, 23:45 UTC**

> Where these rules and the Official Rules on the event page disagree, the Official Rules apply.

---

## 1. Core Principles

1. **You may participate alone or in a team of up to 5 people.**
2. **Every member must be a registered participant.**
3. **Each participant may belong to exactly one team and appear on one submission.**
4. **Teams agree internally on roles, contribution and prize split before submitting.**
5. **Team composition locks when your first submission is created.** Before that, you may join, leave or merge teams freely.
6. **Prizes are split equally among team members by default.** Team size does not change the prize.
7. **Teams resolve their own contribution questions.** Organizers help with process and platform issues but do not arbitrate who contributed more, except where there is misconduct, a policy violation or an eligibility problem.

---

## 2. Participation Formats

### 2.1 Individual

Build and submit alone. Select **"happy to build solo"** in the registration form. You are responsible for choosing a track, building a working agent, meeting the submission requirements and submitting before the deadline.

Solo entries compete in the same pool and for the same prizes as teams.

### 2.2 Pre-formed team

Register as a team you already have. Confirm every member is registered, pick one track, nominate a contact, and create the team on HackOS.

### 2.3 Team formed on HackOS

Register alone, then create or join a team through team-finding on HackOS before the build window opens.

Useful matching criteria:

- Intended track and domain interest
- Complementary skills: backend, ML, data, agents, retrieval, UX, DevOps, evaluation
- **Overlapping availability across time zones.** This is the one people underestimate. A team spread across three continents in a 144-hour sprint needs an explicit handover pattern, not good intentions.
- Experience with agent frameworks
- Agreement on working style and expected hours

## 4. Team Contact

Each team nominates one **primary contact**, and ideally a backup.

This is an administrative role. It does not mean that person owns the idea, the code or a larger share of the prize.

The primary contact:

- Creates the team on HackOS
- Keeps the roster accurate
- Confirms the track
- Coordinates the submission
- Is the point of contact for organizers
- Confirms all members agree to the final submission

A team may change its contact at any time by mutual agreement.

## 6. Settle Your Team Before 22 October

There is no separate merge deadline in a six-day event. The practical deadline is the build window opening.

**Until your first submission is created, you can:** join a team, leave a team, add a registered participant, merge two teams, split off, and change your contact. You can change your track any time before the submission deadline.

**During the build window**, roster changes are still possible until your first submission, but they are costly. Every hour spent renegotiating the roster is an hour not spent building, and you have 144 of them.

**Once your first submission is created**, team composition locks. Make sure the roster is right before you create it.

---

## 7. Adding a Member

A member can be added only if they are a registered participant, not already on another team, and the existing team agrees. The team must stay at five or fewer, and the change must happen before your first submission is created.

**How:** invite through HackOS, the person accepts, the contact confirms the updated roster, and the team grants repository access.

**Before you add someone mid-build, agree:** what they will own, what hours they can give for the days remaining, and how the prize split changes. That last one causes more problems than the first two combined. Someone joining on day four with an equal share is a decision, not a default. Make it explicitly.

---

## 8. Removing a Member

Handle it respectfully and early. If contribution or availability is a problem, raise it on day two, not day six. Roster changes must happen before your first submission is created.

**Valid reasons:** they left voluntarily, they became unavailable, they haven't contributed despite attempts to reach them, they joined another team, they are no longer eligible, they violated the Code of Conduct, or the team mutually agreed.

**Process:** discuss internally, document what was agreed, update the roster on HackOS, update repository access.

If a problem comes up after your team has locked, contact the organizers at hackathons@genai.works with: team name, current roster, the issue, whether the member agrees, and whether repository access or submitted work is affected.

**One thing to be careful about:** removing someone late so they don't share a prize is a conduct issue, not a roster decision. If they contributed and then became unavailable, that is what your prize agreement should have covered.

---

## 9. Merging Teams

Two teams combining into one. Do it **before 22 October**; mid-build merges rarely end well in a six-day window, and they are not possible once either team has created a submission.

Before merging, agree on: the team name, the primary contact, the track, the repository that continues, the architecture direction, member roles, how each team's existing work will be used, credit, **the prize split**, and the submission plan.

**How:** both contacts agree, all members confirm, pick one contact, update the roster on HackOS, close the duplicate team, confirm one track and one repository.

Remember the cap: a merged team still can't exceed **five** members. Two teams of three cannot merge.

---

## 10. Splitting

Before the build window opens you can leave and continue alone, join another team, form a new team, or change track. Update your status on HackOS.

Splitting mid-build is disruptive and hard to do cleanly, particularly around who keeps the repository and the work already in it. It is only possible before your first submission is created. If it becomes necessary, settle repository ownership before anyone starts building separately.

---

## 11. One-Team Rule

Each participant may belong to **one team** and appear on **one submission**. Mentors may advise any number of teams.

You may not: submit substantially the same project through multiple teams, appear on more than one submission, act as a hidden contributor to another team's submission, share private team code without permission, or use another team's repository, logs, demo or architecture without agreement.

Discussion, learning and helping each other on HackOS is encouraged. The submitted work must be the submitting team's own.

---

## 12. Tracks

Every submission selects **exactly one primary track**, which determines the judging panel and the prizes it competes for.

You can change your track any time before the submission deadline from your team workspace. Once you are building, though, a track change costs you time you don't have, so settle it at onboarding if you can.

---

## 13. Repository and Contribution

Teams manage their own process and access.

**Recommended:**

- One shared repository from the start, not on day five
- All active contributors added as collaborators
- Branches and pull requests where the team size justifies them
- Major decisions recorded somewhere: README, issues, a decision log
- **No credentials in the repository, ever**
- `.env.example` with placeholders; `.env` gitignored
- Logs in `logs/`
- Clear documentation of agents, roles, tools and data sources

**On contribution disputes:** organizers won't decide who contributed more unless it involves eligibility, misconduct or plagiarism.

To avoid the dispute, agree on day one: who owns which part, what "active contribution" means, how decisions get made, who submits, and how the prize splits.

---

## 14. Final Roster

Your submission lists all members. The roster should match what's on HackOS, your repository contributors, your README credits and your demo credits.

If it doesn't match, organizers may ask your contact to clarify.

Listing someone without their consent, or leaving out a real contributor deliberately, is a Code of Conduct matter.

---

## 15. Prizes and the Split

The prize pool is **up to $20,000**: $15,000 in cash prizes and $5,000 in in-kind rewards, with a feature in the community showcase for the top project.

**Prizes are split equally among team members by default.**

This is the single most important thing to settle early, and it's the one teams skip. Money is what turns a friendly disagreement on day six into a real dispute on 6 November.

**Agree in writing, before you submit:**

- Whether the split is equal, and if not, what it is
- What happens if someone becomes unavailable partway through
- What happens if someone joins on day four
- Who confirms the split to organizers if you win

Cash prizes are split equally among team members unless the team submits a different split in writing. Organizers pay out on the split the team confirms. If a team can't agree after winning, that's a slow and unpleasant process for everyone. A three-line agreement on day one prevents it.

Cash prizes are paid by bank transfer within 30 days of the results announcement. Taxes and any local charges are the recipient's responsibility.

Employees of the organizer and of judging sponsors may participate but are not eligible for cash prizes. If your team includes one, factor that into the split before you submit.

## 16. Credentials and Teaming

Model choice is open, and every submission integrates Zetaris and Meterless, so credentials are yours to manage. This creates a specific team risk.

**Rules:**

- Never commit a key to the repository
- Environment variables only
- Ship `.env.example` with placeholders
- **The project must not depend on one person's local secrets or manual setup**

That last point is the one that breaks teams. If only one member can run the project because the keys live on their machine, the team is one illness, one time zone or one dead laptop away from not submitting.

**Practical approach:** everyone develops with their own key, the project reads every credential from the environment, and a documented `SAMPLE_MODE=true` runs the whole thing with no credentials at all. That last one is also what lets a judge see your project work.

- [ ] The team is 5 or fewer
- [ ] A primary contact is nominated, with a backup
- [ ] The track is chosen
- [ ] Roles are clear
- [ ] **The prize split is agreed in writing**
- [ ] Repository access is granted to everyone active
- [ ] A communication channel is agreed
- [ ] Time zone coverage and handover are agreed
- [ ] Everyone knows the team locks at the first submission
- [ ] Everyone knows the deadline: **27 October, 23:45 UTC**
